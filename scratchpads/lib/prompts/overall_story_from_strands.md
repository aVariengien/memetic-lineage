## prompt for generating overarching stories from narrative strand archives (v2.1)

---

**your task:** you have been given a set of narrative strands extracted from a community's discourse archive. each strand is a coherent temporal arc—a discourse event with internal structure (trigger → development → outcome). your job is to synthesize these strands into an overarching story that helps the community understand itself.

**what you are NOT doing:**
- writing a wikipedia summary or encyclopedia entry
- forcing a single thesis onto the data
- overindexing on whichever strand appears first in the file
- treating the community as a monolithic entity that "lives" or "dies"

**what you ARE doing:**
- discovering the gravitational centers the scene kept returning to—the recurring questions, tensions, or problems that multiple strands orbit
- identifying currents and their transformations: how did energy flow? what rose, fell, merged, forked, or transformed into something else?
- noticing who and what provided continuity across strands
- articulating any distinctive ways of engaging that characterize the scene
- describing what emerged: tools, vocabulary, practices, experiments, learnings

---

### elements to cover (not necessarily in this order)

**the scene and its gravitational centers:**
- the character of the scene
- recurring questions, tensions, or themes that multiple strands orbit (derive these from the data, don't import them)
- what provided continuity—people, concepts, practices, or other connective tissue
- any distinctive epistemic or social character

**currents and transformations:**
- how the discourse evolved over time
- shifts in energy, focus, or character—not as a single "lifecycle" but as multiple currents that may rise, fall, merge, fork, or transform
- inflection points or environmental changes (platform shifts, external events) that redirected currents
- multiple arcs and turns are expected; don't force everything into one narrative

**what emerged:**
- conceptual tools, vocabulary, or practices that developed
- experiments that were tried, and what they produced
- development arcs (individual or collective) that are visible
- implicit theses the scene kept circling, if any emerge from the data

---

### quality criteria

**grounded, not projected:** every claim should be traceable to specific strands.

**scene-first, not thesis-first:** resist the temptation to find one unifying thesis. multiple gravitational centers > one forced frame.

**continuity without heroes:** note who and what bridges multiple strands, but don't construct a hero narrative.

**currents, not lifecycle:** energy rises and falls, transforms and relocates. avoid framing things as simply "dying"—ask instead where the energy went, what it became, whether it might reconstitute.

**specific over vague:** name specific tools, memes, vocabulary, experiments. "they developed new ideas" is worse than naming the actual ideas.

**appropriate register:** the audience is the community itself, seeking self-understanding.

**length:** scale to the richness of the data. a decade of discourse across hundreds of strands warrants a full essay, not a three-paragraph summary.

---

### anti-patterns to avoid

- **chronological book report:** "in 2015, X happened. then in 2016, Y happened."
- **false coherence:** forcing disparate strands into a single arc when they're parallel or unrelated
- **hero narrative:** "the community was founded by X, who taught everyone Y."
- **premature eulogy:** treating transformation or dispersion as death
- **overindexing on file order:** first strand ≠ origin

---

**output format:** an essay covering the elements above, scaled to the data's richness. conclude with a list identifying which strand IDs most directly support your claims.
